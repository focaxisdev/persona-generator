Welcome—there’s room for both ideas, and a written example can help everyone join in at their own pace.

I’d suggest holding off on twelve tiny slogans: they may compete with the shapes and make a small collage harder to read. The concern that the first draft is confusing is useful, though. Could the group first try clearer placement and, if needed, one short caption? Keep the slogan idea available if others think it helps.

Two feasible approaches:

- **Town:** rectangles become buildings, circles become trees or wheels. Familiar building outlines might make the scene recognizable, but crowded placement could still confuse it.
- **Garden:** circles become flower heads, rectangles become beds or stepping stones, and pencil lines supply stems. Repeated shapes could give it clarity, though flowers may need those drawn details.

**Written example:** “Garden: place three rectangle beds across the bottom. Put two circle flowers above each bed. Draw stems connecting flowers to beds. Use the other three rectangles as a path.” This uses all six circles and six rectangles; scraps can add leaves.

A provisional twenty-minute plan:

1. **Minutes 0–3:** Read the example together, invite concerns, and ask which scene people want to try; no preference is established yet.
2. **Minutes 3–10:** Arrange a first draft loosely.
3. **Minutes 10–12:** Ask what scene each person sees and what remains unclear, allowing written responses.
4. **Minutes 12–17:** Choose either revisions or a second layout; the second costs five minutes.
5. **Minutes 17–20:** Choose and refine one scene together, retaining unresolved disagreements.
