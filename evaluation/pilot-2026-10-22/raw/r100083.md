Welcome—there’s enough time to make a small, playable draft, and everyone’s feedback can help.

I’d suggest keeping the twelve slogans on scrap paper for reference and putting only essential instructions on a larger, readable sheet. A small card packed with slogans may be difficult to scan. The confusion is useful feedback: ask which step was unclear. Provide a written turn example before explaining aloud, and allow quiet reading time.

Two feasible options:

- **Matching:** Draw circles on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle face down; the player turns over two cards, winning if they match. Otherwise, reveal all three and reset. This offers a simple memory/chance game, though one round has little variety.
- **Shape sorting:** Draw a circle, triangle, and square. Shuffle face up; the player wins by arranging them from fewest to most corners. This is easy to demonstrate, though it may become predictable quickly.

Written example for sorting: “Start: square, circle, triangle. Move circle first, triangle second, square third. Check: 0, 3, 4 corners—you win.”

Provisional twenty-minute plan:

1. **0–3:** Hear concerns and choose either option together; neither is an established preference.
2. **3–7:** Make three cards and write setup, turn, win condition, and example on scrap paper.
3. **7–12:** Read, play, and note unclear steps.
4. **12–17:** Revise, or spend five minutes trying the alternative if the group chooses.
5. **17–20:** Select and replay one draft, recording any unresolved disagreement for later.
