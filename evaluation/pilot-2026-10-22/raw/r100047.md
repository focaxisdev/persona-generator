Welcome—let’s make a scene everyone can help shape within twenty minutes. These are suggestions for your group to accept or change.

The confusing first draft is useful feedback. I’d suggest holding off on twelve tiny slogans: they may compete with the shapes and make a small collage harder to read. We could first clarify the arrangement, then consider one short caption if it still helps. Anyone who favors the slogans can explain what they would clarify.

Thanks for asking for a written example. Keep this beside the work so nobody has to rely on a fast spoken explanation:

**Town example:** Put four rectangles together as buildings, with four circles above them as treetops. Use the remaining two rectangles as tree trunks and two circles as a sun and pond. Pencil in windows and a road; use scraps for roofs.

A town offers familiar building and street cues. A garden could use circles as flowers and rectangles as beds or paths. Either may work; we don’t know the group’s preference. Trying both costs five minutes that could otherwise improve one scene.

Suggested session:

- **0–3 minutes:** Read the example, invite concerns, and choose a starting scene together.
- **3–8:** Arrange loose shapes.
- **8–13:** Either try the alternative layout or improve the first, as the group chooses.
- **13–18:** Refine the chosen scene with scraps and pencil details.
- **18–20:** Ask what scene people recognize, make one useful adjustment, and stop.
