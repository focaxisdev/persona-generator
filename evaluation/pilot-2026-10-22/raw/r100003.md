Welcome, collage crew! You have enough materials to make a recognizable scene, and the confusing first draft gives you something useful to improve.

I’d suggest testing clearer shapes and spacing before adding twelve tiny slogans. Those captions might crowd the small collage and make reading harder, though someone may see a useful role for them. You could compare an uncaptioned version with one short title on a removable scrap. The group should decide what helps.

Thanks for asking for a written example—keep this beside the materials so nobody has to follow a fast explanation:

- **Town:** Six rectangles become buildings; six circles become tree tops. Pencil in trunks, doors, and one road. For example: “Buildings above the road; trees along its edge.” Familiar features may help recognition, but crowded buildings could still confuse.
- **Garden:** Six rectangles become planting beds; six circles become flowers or bushes. A penciled path connects them. Repetition could make the arrangement clear, but viewers might need distinguishing details.

Neither layout has an established preference advantage. A second attempt costs five minutes.

A provisional twenty-minute plan:

1. **0–2:** Read the example together, invite concerns, and choose a starting scene.
2. **2–7:** Arrange loose pieces and add a few pencil details.
3. **7–10:** Ask what people recognize and what remains confusing; allow written feedback.
4. **10–15:** Choose either a second layout or further refinement.
5. **15–20:** Select the preferred draft, simplify it, and check readability.

You can make useful progress without everyone agreeing; keep dissent visible and let the group make the final choice.
