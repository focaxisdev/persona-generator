For the participant who finds fast explanations difficult to follow, I suggest providing a written example and quiet reading time before discussion:

**Lost-and-found example**
1. Draw an empty pocket: “Mia’s key is missing.”
2. Draw a key beside a bench: “She checks where she sat.”
3. Draw Mia holding the key: “Found! Now she can go home.”

A lost-and-found story offers a concrete problem, search, and resolution. A journey-home story could show departure, a clear landmark, and arrival. Both are feasible with pencils and scrap paper; neither has an established preference advantage here. Trying both costs five minutes that could otherwise improve readability.
