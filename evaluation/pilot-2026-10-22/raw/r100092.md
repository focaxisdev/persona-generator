Welcome—there’s room to try ideas and keep this small.

The concern that the first draft is confusing is useful. Twelve narration slogans might explain more, but I suspect they would crowd the opening panel and make it harder to read. A provisional alternative is one short caption per panel, with the drawings carrying the action. Keep the twelve slogans on scrap paper so their author can suggest which details matter most.

Both stories are feasible:

- **Lost-and-found:** a missing object, a search, and a return give each panel a clear job.
- **Journey home:** a departure, an obstacle, and an arrival can also work, though the obstacle may need a clear visual cue.

Neither is known to be the group’s preference. Trying both gives you a comparison but costs five minutes that could go toward making one clearer.

Here’s a written lost-and-found example to review at your own pace:

1. Empty hook: “My hat is missing.”
2. Hat beside a bench: “There it is!”
3. Person wearing hat: “Found it.”

One possible twenty-minute plan:

- **Minutes 0–3:** Read the example together; choose a story and agree what happens in each panel.
- **3–8:** Make a rough sketch. If you choose to compare stories, use this slot for the second thumbnail.
- **8–15:** Draft the chosen display.
- **15–18:** Invite someone to describe what they understand; welcome disagreements and written comments.
- **18–20:** Revise the confusing part.

Which story and approach would the group choose?
