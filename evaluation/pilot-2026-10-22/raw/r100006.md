Welcome—there’s room to make this simple and include everyone’s feedback.

I’d suggest keeping the twelve slogans on scrap paper for now. Crowding them onto one small card may make the confusing draft harder to follow. We could choose the few rules needed for one round and put a written example beside them. The request for that example is useful feedback; a fast verbal explanation needn’t be the only way to learn.

Two provisional options fit the same twenty minutes and twelve-card limit:

- **Matching:** Assuming repeated shapes are acceptable, draw a circle on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle them face up; the player selects the matching pair. This needs little explanation, though it may offer limited replay interest.
- **Sorting:** Assuming size differences are easy to draw and distinguish, draw a small, medium, and large circle on three cards. Shuffle them; the player arranges them smallest to largest. This offers a clear sequence, though uneven drawings could cause disagreement.

**Written example:** “Cards show ○, △, ○. Pick the two circles. A round ends when the pair is selected.”

A possible session plan:
1. **Minutes 0–3:** Let everyone comment, then choose one option provisionally; preference is currently unknown.
2. **3–7:** Make three cards and write the goal, actions, ending, and example on scrap.
3. **7–12:** Try rounds, allowing time to read; note confusion and dissent.
4. **12–17:** Revise, or spend the known five minutes trying the alternative.
5. **17–20:** The group chooses one draft and checks its written instructions.
