Welcome—there’s room for everyone to help make a clear little story.

The concern that the first draft is confusing is useful. Twelve narration slogans might crowd the small opening panel and make the sequence harder to follow. Could the group try one short caption per panel, with drawings carrying the action? Keep the extra wording on scrap paper in case someone wants to revisit it.

For the participant who wants a written example, leave this where everyone can read it at their own pace:

1. A person spots a scarf on a bench. “Someone lost this.”
2. They hold it up; another person turns back. “Is this yours?”
3. The owner takes it and smiles. “Thank you!”

A lost-and-found story offers a simple problem and visible resolution. A journey-home story could show departure, a recognizable landmark, and arrival; it may need clearer location cues. Either could work, and we don’t know which the group prefers. Trying both costs five minutes that could otherwise improve readability.

One possible twenty-minute plan:

- **Minutes 0–3:** Read the example, invite concerns and preferences, and choose a story together. If views differ, agree on a temporary first choice.
- **Minutes 3–8:** Roughly sketch all three panels.
- **Minutes 8–10:** Ask someone to describe the sequence from the page; note confusing parts without blaming anyone.
- **Minutes 10–15:** Revise, or spend the five minutes trying the alternative.
- **Minutes 15–20:** Choose together, simplify captions, and finish the display.

The fictional group can adapt this plan and make the final choice.
