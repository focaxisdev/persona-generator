Welcome—there’s enough here to make a small, playable draft! The feedback and request for a written example give the group useful starting points.

I’d suggest keeping the twelve slogans on scrap paper for now. On one small card, they may be hard to scan, especially while learning. Could the group reduce the playable rules to a few short steps and put a written example beside them? The participant’s concern deserves a response: allow quiet reading before a slow walkthrough, with time for questions.

Two feasible options:

- **Matching pairs:** Draw two circles and one triangle on three cards. Lay them face up; the player selects the two matching shapes. Selecting both circles wins the round. This offers a short rule, though it may feel too obvious.
- **Shape sorting:** Draw a circle, triangle, and square. Arrange them from fewest to most straight sides: circle → triangle → square. This gives a clear written example, though the sorting criterion needs explanation.

Neither option is known to be the group’s preference. Here’s a provisional twenty-minute plan for them to accept or change:

- **0–3:** Hear preferences and concerns; choose a starting option.
- **3–7:** Draw three cards and write rules plus one example on scrap paper.
- **7–12:** Read, walk through, and try a round; note confusion and dissent.
- **12–17:** Choose either revisions or a second prototype; the latter costs five minutes.
- **17–20:** Confirm one playable draft and record unresolved questions.
