Welcome—there’s room for both the critique and different ways of following along. A useful model is **scene first, details second**: a few clear shapes establish the subject; words and decoration help only if they strengthen that first impression.

I’d take “confusing” seriously, while provisionally suggesting we hold off on twelve tiny slogans. They may compete with the scene. Could we first identify which part is unclear? A teammate may still think captions help; one short, readable label is a reversible option to test.

Neither scene has an evidenced preference advantage:

- **Town:** rectangles can become buildings, with circles as trees or wheels. It may offer clear landmarks but needs spacing so buildings don’t merge.
- **Garden:** circles can become flowers and rectangles beds or a path. It may be simple to arrange, though flowers could look like unrelated dots without stems and grouping.

**Written town example:** “Place three rectangles along the bottom as houses. Pencil in doors and roofs. Put two circles above narrow scrap-paper trunks beside the houses. Leave a clear strip across the bottom as a street.” Unused shapes can stay unused. This gives everyone something concrete to inspect at their own pace.

A provisional twenty-minute plan: 3 minutes to read this example and choose together; 7 to arrange a first scene; 2 to hear what each person recognizes, including dissent; 5 to try the alternative if wanted, or refine the first; 3 to choose and simplify. Switching costs five minutes. The group keeps final judgment.
