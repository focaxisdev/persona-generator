Welcome—let’s aim for one small, playable game everyone can follow.

The twelve-slogan card could collect useful ideas, but I share the concern that it would be crowded and hard to read. Provisionally, keep the three playing cards simple and put a short rule plus a written example on scrap paper. The request for a written example is useful feedback; a fast spoken explanation alone may not give everyone enough time.

Two feasible options:

- **Find the pair:** Draw a circle on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle them face up; the player points to the matching pair. This has a short rule, though it may offer little replay variety with only three cards.
- **Shape sorting:** Draw a circle, triangle, and square. Ask players to arrange them by number of straight sides. Written example: “Circle: 0; triangle: 3; square: 4. Place them in that order.” This gives a clear sequence, though the sorting criterion needs explaining.

There’s no evidence about which the group prefers. Either could be a reasonable first draft.

A suggested twenty-minute plan:

1. **Minutes 0–3:** Hear brief preferences and choose a first option together.
2. **3–7:** Make three cards, one short written rule, and an example.
3. **7–11:** Try a round, allowing silent reading time; note confusion and differing views.
4. **11–16:** Either revise, or spend the known five minutes trying the second prototype.
5. **16–20:** Choose together, clarify the rule, and play one final round.
