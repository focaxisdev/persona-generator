Welcome—let’s make a clear little story together. These are provisional suggestions for the group to choose or change.

The concern that the first draft is confusing deserves attention. Twelve slogans might explain more, but I suspect they would crowd the small opening panel and make it harder to read. Could we try one short caption there and spread essential information across the pictures? Keep the slogans on scrap paper so their useful ideas remain available.

Thanks for asking for a written example; a fast spoken explanation may not give everyone enough time. Here is one to read and mark up:

1. **Find:** A person spots a scarf on a bench. Caption: “Someone left this behind.”
2. **Seek:** They hold it up near the bench. “Is this yours?”
3. **Return:** Its owner takes it. “Thank you!”

A lost-and-found story offers a concrete object and visible resolution. A journey-home story can also work: show departure, a recognizable obstacle, and arrival. Neither is known to be the group’s preference. Trying both costs five minutes but could help if choosing remains difficult.

Suggested twenty-minute plan:

- **0–3:** Read the example silently, collect written reactions, and choose a story together; record any unresolved concern.
- **3–8:** Sketch three small panels on scrap. If the group chooses to compare stories, use these five minutes for both rough versions.
- **8–15:** Draft the chosen display with sparse text.
- **15–20:** Invite someone to read it without explanation, revise confusing parts, and let the group judge whether it is ready.
