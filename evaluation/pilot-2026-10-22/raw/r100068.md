Welcome! A useful model is **one action, one clear goal, one worked example**: the three cards provide the play, while scrap paper explains it. This could help the group judge both options without assuming anyone’s preference.

I’d suggest moving the twelve slogans onto scrap paper and reducing them to the few rules needed for a first turn. Twelve slogans on one small card may be difficult to scan. The concern about fast explanations deserves a written example that people can read at their own pace; a verbal explanation can accompany it.

Use the same three cards for comparison: draw a circle on two cards and a triangle on the third.

- **Matching:** Shuffle them face down. Reveal two; two circles win, otherwise turn them back and try again. Written example: “Circle + triangle: try again. Circle + circle: win.” This adds memory and chance, though three cards may offer little challenge.
- **Sorting:** Shuffle them face up. Arrange circles before triangles. Written example: “Triangle, circle, circle → circle, circle, triangle.” This makes the goal visible, though it may feel more like a puzzle than a game.

A provisional twenty-minute plan:

1. **2 minutes:** Hear preferences and choose a starting option; either remains reasonable.
2. **4 minutes:** Draw three cards and write rules plus the example.
3. **6 minutes:** Try playing from the written instructions; revise confusing parts.
4. **5 minutes:** If the group chooses, try the second prototype; otherwise improve the first.
5. **3 minutes:** Choose one playable draft, noting unresolved concerns.
