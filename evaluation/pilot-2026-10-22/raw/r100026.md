Welcome. Here is a provisional approach for the group to consider.

The concern about the first draft is useful. Twelve slogans on one small card may make the rules harder to scan. I suggest keeping the three playing cards simple and putting a short rule sheet and written example on scrap paper. A verbal explanation can accompany that sheet, with time to read and ask questions.

Two feasible options are:

- **Matching pairs:** Draw circles on two cards and a square on the third. Shuffle them face down. One player chooses and reveals two cards; matching shapes win, different shapes lose. Shuffle before each new round. This has a clear outcome, but offers little strategy.
- **Shape sorting:** Draw a circle, square, and triangle. Give a written target order, scramble the cards, and have a player arrange them to match. This is easy to check, though it may feel more like a puzzle than a competitive game.

**Written matching example:** “You reveal a circle and a square: you lose this round. Shuffle all three cards and try again.”

A possible twenty-minute plan:

- **0–3:** Let everyone indicate a preference, including concerns; agree how to choose if opinions differ.
- **3–8:** Draw three cards and write rules plus an example.
- **8–12:** Let someone try using the written sheet; record confusing steps.
- **12–17:** Choose either revisions or a second prototype, acknowledging its five-minute cost.
- **17–20:** Retest and agree on the playable version.

Neither option has evidence of group preference; the group retains the choice.
