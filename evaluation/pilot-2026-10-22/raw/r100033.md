Welcome—there’s room here for different ideas and a readable first draft.

The confusing draft is useful feedback. I’d suggest holding off on twelve tiny slogans: they might clarify individual details, but they could crowd the collage and make the overall scene harder to read. If someone favors captions, the group could compare a few large labels with an unlabelled version. The group should decide.

Here’s a written example to keep available while discussing: **Town:** place three rectangles as buildings, add pencil doors and windows, and use two circles as trees beside a scrap-paper road. Leave the other shapes available until the scene is clear.

Two feasible approaches:

- **Develop one layout:** spend more time making either town or garden recognizable. A garden could use rectangles as beds and circles as flowers, with pencil stems. This saves time but offers less comparison.
- **Try both briefly:** rearrange the loose pieces to compare town and garden. This may reveal a clearer composition; the known downside is that the second layout uses five of the twenty minutes.

A provisional twenty-minute plan:

1. **Minutes 0–3:** read the example together, hear concerns, and choose one layout or a two-layout comparison. Neither scene has an established preference.
2. **Minutes 3–8:** draft the first scene.
3. **Minutes 8–13:** try the second layout if chosen; otherwise refine the first.
4. **Minutes 13–18:** choose and simplify, allowing disagreement.
5. **Minutes 18–20:** check recognizability and caption readability together; leave final judgment with the group.
