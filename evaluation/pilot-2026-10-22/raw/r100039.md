Welcome—there’s enough here to make a small, playable draft together.

The twelve slogans may capture useful ideas, but fitting them on one small card could make the rules hard to read. A fast verbal explanation also leaves the participant who requested a written example without the support they asked for. One option is to put a short rule and a worked example on scrap paper, keeping the three playing cards uncluttered. The teammate can help choose which slogans become essential instructions.

Two feasible approaches are:

- **Matching:** Draw circles on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle them face down; the player turns over two. Matching shapes win; otherwise, turn them back and try again. This offers a simple memory challenge.
- **Sorting:** Draw one circle, triangle, and square. Shuffle them face up; the player arranges them by number of sides. Written example: “Square, circle, triangle → circle, triangle, square: 0, 3, 4 sides.” This makes the action visible throughout.

We don’t know which the group prefers. Provisionally, the group could use this twenty-minute plan:

1. **Minutes 0–3:** Choose an approach and agree on its goal.
2. **3–7:** Draw three cards; write the rule and example.
3. **7–12:** Let someone try using the written instructions; collect confusing points and differing views.
4. **12–17:** Revise, or spend five minutes trying the alternative.
5. **17–20:** Choose one draft, clarify its instructions, and play once more.

The group makes the final choice.
