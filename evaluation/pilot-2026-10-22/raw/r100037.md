Welcome—there’s enough here to make a small, playable draft together.

The twelve slogans might help collect ideas, but I’m concerned they’ll be hard to read on one small card. Could we keep them on scrap paper and put only essential play information on the three game cards? A written example could stay beside the game so nobody has to rely on a fast verbal explanation.

Two feasible options:

- **Matching:** Draw a circle on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle them face down; turn over two. Matching shapes win; otherwise reset and try again. This offers a simple hidden-information game, though three cards provide little variety.
- **Sorting:** Draw one, two, and three shapes on the cards. Shuffle them face up; arrange them from fewest shapes to most. This is easy to demonstrate and check, though it may feel more like a puzzle than a game.

Usable written matching example: “I turn over a circle and a triangle. They differ, so I turn both back down. Next turn, I try to find the two circles.”

A provisional twenty-minute plan:

1. **Minutes 0–3:** Choose an option and agree on its goal.
2. **3–7:** Draw three cards; write rules and an example on scrap paper.
3. **7–12:** Try playing with the written instructions; note confusion.
4. **12–17:** Revise, or spend five minutes trying the second prototype.
5. **17–20:** Retest and select one playable draft.

Which option—and whether to reserve five minutes for the alternative—would your group choose?
