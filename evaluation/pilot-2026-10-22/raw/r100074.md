Welcome—there’s room to make a small game everyone can follow. These are provisional suggestions for the fictional group to choose.

Twelve slogans on one small card may be hard to read and may not resolve the confusing parts. I’d suggest keeping three playing cards and writing a short rule plus a worked example on scrap paper. The request for a written example can guide the explanation; invite corrections without requiring anyone to follow a fast verbal summary.

Two feasible approaches:
- **Matching:** Draw a circle on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle face down; a player turns over two cards and wins if they match. Otherwise, reveal all three and start another round. This is simple but offers little choice.
- **Sorting:** Draw one, two, and three triangles on separate cards. Shuffle face up; arrange them from fewest to most. Example: **3, 1, 2 → 1, 2, 3** completes a round. This avoids remembering hidden cards but may feel too easy.

Suggested twenty-minute checklist:
- [ ] **Minutes 0–3:** Ask which approach to try; record disagreement. With no preference evidence, either is a tentative starting point.
- [ ] **3–7:** Make three cards and written instructions, including setup, action, and round ending.
- [ ] **7–12:** Try a round using the written example. Check what remains confusing.
- [ ] **12–17:** Clarify and replay, or spend these five minutes trying the alternative if the first approach disappoints.
- [ ] **17–20:** Let the group choose a draft, verify it plays, and note unresolved issues.
