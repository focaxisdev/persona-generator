Welcome, everyone—the known downside is that trying a second prototype takes five of your twenty minutes, leaving less time to improve the first.

I’d suggest keeping the twelve slogans on scrap paper for now. A single small card may be hard to read, and more slogans may not resolve the confusing parts. For the three game cards, try large, clear drawings; put the short rules and a written example alongside them. The request for a written example is useful: allow quiet reading before explaining aloud, and keep the example visible during play.

Both approaches are feasible:

- **Matching pairs:** Draw a circle on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle face down; reveal two. Matching shapes win; otherwise turn them back and try again. Written example: “Circle + triangle: turn both back. Circle + circle: you win.” This offers a simple memory challenge, though three cards give little variety.
- **Sorting shapes:** Draw a circle, triangle, and square. Shuffle, then arrange them by number of sides: 0, 3, 4. This is easy to check, though repeated play may become predictable.
