Welcome—there is room to make a clear story together in this short session.

The first draft has not yet made the sequence clear to everyone. Twelve narration slogans might explain more, but I think they could crowd the small opening panel and make it harder to read. Could we provisionally try one brief caption per panel, with the drawings carrying the main action? Keep the extra wording on scrap paper in case it helps later.

A written example would let people review the idea at their own pace:

1. **Lost:** A person looks at an empty pocket. “Where is my key?”
2. **Found:** Someone spots the key beside a bench. “Here it is.”
3. **Returned:** The key reaches its owner. “Thank you!”

Lost-and-found offers a concrete problem and visible resolution. Journey-home could also work: someone leaves, encounters a blocked path, and finds another route home. Neither is known to be the group’s preference. Developing one leaves more time for readability; trying the second costs five minutes but offers a useful comparison if the first remains unclear.

One possible twenty-minute plan:

- **0–3:** Share the written example, allow quiet reading, and invite preferences or concerns. Choose a provisional story together.
- **3–8:** Sketch three rough panels.
- **8–10:** Ask whether the sequence is understandable without verbal explanation.
- **10–15:** Revise, or spend five minutes trying the alternative.
- **15–20:** Choose together and finish readable drawings and captions.

The group can adapt this plan and make the final choice.
