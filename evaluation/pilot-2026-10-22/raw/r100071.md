Welcome—there’s room for both clearer rules and a written example.

I disagree with putting twelve rule slogans on one small card: I think it will be hard to read and could make the confusing draft harder to follow. Could we try a few short steps on scrap paper instead? A fast verbal explanation may leave someone behind, so I suggest keeping a written example visible and allowing time to read it.

Two feasible three-card drafts are:

- **Matching:** Draw a circle on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle them face down. A player turns over two; matching shapes win, while a mismatch is turned back for another attempt. This needs little writing but asks players to remember positions.
- **Sorting:** Draw one, two, and three circles on three cards. Shuffle them face up; the player arranges them from fewest circles to most. This keeps everything visible but may feel too simple.

Written sorting example: “Start: three circles, one circle, two circles. Finish: one circle, two circles, three circles.”

A provisional twenty-minute plan:

1. **3 minutes:** Hear concerns and choose one draft; we have no evidence of a shared preference.
2. **5 minutes:** Make three cards and write the steps and example.
3. **4 minutes:** Let someone try using the written instructions; note confusing moments.
4. **5 minutes:** Choose either revisions or a second prototype. Trying both costs these five minutes.
5. **3 minutes:** Replay the chosen draft and record its playable rules.

The fictional group can disagree and make the final choice.
