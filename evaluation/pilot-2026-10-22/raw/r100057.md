Twelve slogans on one small card would likely make reading harder; I’d suggest keeping the rules and a written example on scrap paper, with the three game cards showing only what play needs. The group can choose otherwise.

A written example seems useful here: the participant has explicitly asked for one, and a fast explanation hasn’t worked for them. Let everyone read before inviting questions or disagreement.

Two feasible options:

- **Matching:** Draw circles on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle them face up; the player selects the matching pair. Example: “Circle, triangle, circle → choose the two circles; that completes the round.” This needs little explanation, though it may feel too easy.
- **Sorting:** Draw a circle, triangle, and square. Write a target order on scrap paper; shuffle the cards and have the player arrange them to match. This offers a different task, but the target order needs to remain visible.
