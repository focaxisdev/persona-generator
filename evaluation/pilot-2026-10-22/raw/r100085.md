The captions could add humor and help explain details. I’d nevertheless gently disagree with adding twelve tiny slogans: on a small collage, they may compete with the shapes and make the scene harder to read. We could provisionally try no captions or one short, clearly written label.

Thanks for asking for a written example. We could keep this pencil note beside the collage so nobody has to rely on a fast spoken explanation:

“Town example: place six rectangles along a scrap-paper road as buildings. Use six circles as tree crowns, with scrap-paper trunks. Leave space between buildings and trees.”

Two feasible approaches:

- **Develop one layout:** choose either that town or a garden with six rectangular beds and six circular flowers. This preserves more time for spacing and clarity, but offers less comparison.
- **Try both:** make one draft, then rearrange it into the other scene. This provides a concrete comparison, but the second layout costs five minutes. We don’t know which scene the group prefers.

A possible twenty-minute plan, for the group to accept or change:

1. **2 minutes:** read the example together and choose a starting scene.
2. **5 minutes:** arrange the shapes loosely.
3. **3 minutes:** invite each person to say or write what they recognize and what confuses them.
4. **5 minutes:** refine that draft or try the second layout.
5. **5 minutes:** choose the clearest available arrangement, simplify details, and finish the draft.

Keep disagreements visible; the group makes the final choice.
