Welcome. Here is a provisional approach for the group to consider.

The concern that the first draft is confusing is useful feedback. I would suggest holding off on twelve tiny slogans: they could crowd the small collage and compete with the scene. Someone may still prefer captions; a compromise is to test one short title after the shapes are recognizable.

Both layouts are feasible:
- A familiar town can use rectangles as buildings and circles as wheels, trees, or a sun. Clear placement may help recognition, though too many objects could become cluttered.
- A garden can use circles as flower heads and rectangles as beds or paths, with pencil stems. Repetition could make it coherent, though it might need a clear path to establish the scene.

Written example: “Town: place three rectangles along the bottom as buildings. Put two circles beside one rectangle as trees and one circle above as the sun. Pencil in doors and windows.” There is no need to use every shape.

A possible twenty-minute plan:
1. Minutes 0–3: Write both options, read the example together, and invite preferences or concerns. Choose provisionally; neither scene has established support.
2. Minutes 3–10: Arrange loose shapes and add a few pencil details.
3. Minutes 10–12: Ask what scene people recognize and what remains confusing; allow written replies.
4. Minutes 12–17: Either simplify the first layout or spend five minutes trying the alternative.
5. Minutes 17–20: Let the group choose a draft and add only helpful details.
