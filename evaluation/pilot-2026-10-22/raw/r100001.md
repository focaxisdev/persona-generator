The concern that the first draft is confusing is useful. I’d hesitate to put twelve slogans in the small opening panel: they may make it harder to read. Could we keep that suggestion on scrap paper and try one short caption instead? That is a provisional readability guess, not a verdict. Let’s also write an example and allow quiet reading time so participation doesn’t depend on following a fast explanation.

Assuming the display needs to stand alone and simple pencil drawings are sufficient, three feasible approaches fit the same twenty-minute budget:

- **Lost-and-found only:** Spend the available drafting time making one clear sequence. Example: panel 1, “Mia’s scarf is missing”; panel 2, Mia retraces her steps; panel 3, “Found it on the bench!”
- **Journey-home only:** Show a character leaving, encountering a recognizable obstacle, and arriving home. This offers a different plot without spending time comparing drafts.
- **Compare both:** Use five minutes to sketch a second story. This gives the group concrete alternatives but leaves five fewer minutes for refinement; it may or may not be worthwhile.

Suggested session: minutes 0–3, read the written example and choose an approach; 3–12, sketch, including any five-minute alternative trial; 12–16, invite someone to describe what they understand and hear dissent; 16–20, revise and assemble. Keep explanations written alongside sketches. The fictional group chooses the story and any changes; neither preference nor clarity is established yet.
