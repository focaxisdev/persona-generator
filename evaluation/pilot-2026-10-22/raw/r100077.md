**The first draft is confusing: that is a real setback for this twenty-minute session.** It gives us a useful revision target, though it does not establish that captions are the solution. I would suggest postponing the twelve tiny slogans: they may crowd the small collage and make its main shapes harder to recognize. Others may disagree; we could keep their wording ideas on scrap paper for later consideration.

Here is a written example to keep beside the collage: “Town: use three rectangles as buildings, three as doors, and six circles as trees or wheels. Leave space between objects.” We can pause for reading and questions rather than rely on a fast explanation.

Both approaches seem feasible:

- A town offers buildings and streets as recognition cues.
- A garden could use circles as flower heads and rectangles as beds, with penciled stems.

Neither has an evidenced preference advantage. Switching layouts costs five minutes, so I would provisionally favor choosing one together and revising it first.

A possible plan:

1. Minutes 0–3: read the example, hear concerns, and choose town or garden.
2. Minutes 3–10: arrange the twelve shapes loosely; pencil only essential details.
3. Minutes 10–15: check what each person recognizes; revise, or spend these five minutes trying the alternative.
4. Minutes 15–20: choose the clearer draft together and finish essential details.

The group can adapt this plan and make the final choice.
