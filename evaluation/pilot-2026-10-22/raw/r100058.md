Thanks for flagging that the first draft is confusing. I suggest first adjusting the shapes and spacing, then comparing that with a small caption sample. If someone favors the slogans, they could pencil two examples on removable scraps so the group can judge their usefulness. A written example can stay beside the collage for anyone who finds a fast verbal explanation difficult to follow.

Both scene options are feasible:

- **Town:** rectangles can become buildings; circles can become trees or wheels. Familiar landmarks may help recognition, though crowded shapes could remain confusing.
- **Garden:** rectangles can become beds or paths; circles can become flowers. Repeated shapes may clarify the scene, though flowers might need pencil stems to be recognizable.

**Written example:** “Town: place three rectangle buildings above a scrap-paper road. Add two circle treetops with pencil trunks. Leave space between buildings.” You need not use every shape.

A provisional twenty-minute plan:

- **0–2:** Read the example, hear preferences, and choose a scene together.
- **2–7:** Arrange a loose first draft.
- **7–9:** Ask what is recognizable and what remains confusing; allow written feedback.
- **9–14:** Try the other layout if the group chooses, or refine the first.
- **14–19:** Select and clarify the layout; compare caption scraps.
- **19–20:** Check readability and record any unresolved disagreement.
