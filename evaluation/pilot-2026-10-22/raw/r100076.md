Welcome—there’s room here for both a clearer draft and different ways of following the rules.

The twelve slogans might capture useful ideas, but I’d suggest keeping them on scrap paper as a reference: squeezing them onto one small card could make the confusion harder to resolve. The request for a written example is useful feedback. Offer one alongside a brief verbal explanation, with time to read and ask questions.

Two feasible three-card prototypes are:

- **Matching:** Draw a circle on two cards and a triangle on the third. Lay all three face up; the player selects the matching pair and wins if the shapes agree. This has a simple endpoint, though little challenge in this first version.
- **Sorting:** Draw one, two, and three circles on separate cards. Shuffle them; the player arranges them from fewest to most circles. This gives a clear sequence, though it tests counting rather than matching.

A written example could say: “Starting order: three circles, one circle, two circles. Finished order: one, two, three.”

A provisional twenty-minute plan:

1. **Minutes 0–3:** Invite preferences and choose one prototype; neither has demonstrated group support.
2. **3–7:** Draw three cards and write the goal, action, finish condition, and example on scrap paper.
3. **7–12:** Let someone try using the written instructions; note confusing steps.
4. **12–17:** Revise and retry, or spend the known five minutes trying the alternative if the group chooses.
5. **17–20:** Select and clarify one playable version, recording any unresolved disagreement.

The fictional group retains the final choice.
