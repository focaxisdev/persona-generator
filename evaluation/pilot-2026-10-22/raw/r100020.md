The twelve slogans could add humor and help explain the story. Still, I’d lean against adding all twelve to a small collage: they may compete with the shapes and make the scene harder to recognize. That’s a provisional judgment; someone may see a useful role for a caption.

Thanks for asking for a written example. We could keep the instructions on scrap paper so nobody has to rely on a fast verbal explanation:

“Town example: put three rectangles along the bottom as buildings, three above them as roofs or signs, and arrange six circles as treetops, wheels, or a sun. Pencil in doors and a road.”

A town offers familiar building and road cues. A garden could use circles as flowers and rectangles as beds, with penciled stems and paths. Either seems feasible; we don’t know which the group prefers. Clear placement with perhaps one short caption leaves more space than twelve slogans, though it offers less written storytelling.

One possible twenty-minute plan:

- Minutes 0–2: read the example, invite questions, and choose town or garden together.
- Minutes 2–10: arrange the shapes loosely and add pencil details.
- Minutes 10–15: review recognizability; choose either refinement or a second layout, acknowledging its five-minute cost.
- Minutes 15–20: finish the chosen scene and consider one caption if helpful.

The group can adapt this plan, keep disagreements visible, and make the final choice.
