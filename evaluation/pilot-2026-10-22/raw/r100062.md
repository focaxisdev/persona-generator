Welcome—there’s room to make this clear together in twenty minutes.

The concern that the first draft is confusing is useful. I’d suggest holding off on twelve tiny slogans: they may compete with the shapes and make a small collage harder to read. That’s a provisional judgment; captions could help if a particular object remains ambiguous. A single short label is another viable option.

Both scenes are feasible. A town could use rectangles for buildings and circles for trees or wheels; a garden could use circles for flowers and rectangles for beds or a path. Neither is known to be the group’s preference. Staying with one layout preserves working time; trying the other offers a comparison but costs five minutes.

For the participant who wants writing, offer this pencil-written example and leave time to read it: “Garden: arrange three circles above three narrow rectangles as flowers; place the remaining shapes beside a scrap-paper path.” Explain verbally too, if helpful, without requiring anyone to keep pace with speech.

One possible plan for the group to choose:

- Minutes 0–3: Read the example, hear concerns, and choose town or garden.
- Minutes 3–8: Arrange a loose first layout.
- Minutes 8–10: Ask what scene each person recognizes and what confuses them; keep differing views visible.
- Minutes 10–15: Revise, or spend these five minutes testing the alternative.
- Minutes 15–20: Choose and refine one draft, adding a short label only if useful.

The group makes the final choice.
