Welcome—there’s room to make a small game everyone can follow. These suggestions are provisional; the fictional group can choose or revise them.

I’d suggest keeping twelve slogans off one small card: they may be difficult to read and could leave the confusion unresolved. Could we put a few short rules and a written example on scrap paper instead? Thank you to the participant who asked for that example; allow quiet reading time before inviting questions.

Two feasible approaches:
- **Matching pairs:** Draw circles on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle them face down; a player turns over two, winning if the shapes match. This offers a simple reveal-and-check turn.
- **Sorting shapes:** Draw a circle, triangle, and square. A player arranges them by increasing number of corners. Written example: “Circle: 0 → triangle: 3 → square: 4.” This makes the target visible, though it may feel more like a puzzle.

We don’t know which the group prefers. A quick choice can start the rehearsal without claiming consensus.

Suggested completion checkpoints:
1. **Minutes 0–3:** Choose an approach; record any dissent.
2. **3–7:** Make three cards and write the goal, turn, ending, and one example.
3. **7–12:** Let someone read, then attempt a full play; note confusing steps.
4. **12–17:** Revise, or spend the known five minutes trying the alternative if the group chooses.
5. **17–20:** Select one draft and check it once.

**Stop at twenty minutes:** keep one playable draft if available; otherwise record the unresolved rule without calling it finished.
