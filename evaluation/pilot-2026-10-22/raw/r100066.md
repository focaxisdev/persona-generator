Welcome—there’s room to make this small and playable together.

I’d suggest keeping the twelve slogans on scrap paper for now. On one small card, they may be hard to read, and they could crowd out the game itself. The confusion is useful feedback: could the group reduce the rules to a goal, a turn, and an ending? For the participant who finds a fast explanation difficult to follow, offer a written example and time to read it before inviting questions.

Two feasible options:

- **Matching pairs:** Draw a circle on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle them face down. Turn over two; matching circles win, otherwise turn them back and try again. This adds memory and a reveal step.
- **Sorting shapes:** Use the same three cards face up. Arrange them by number of corners: circle, circle, triangle. Either circle can come first. This has fewer steps, though the group may find it too simple.

There’s no evidence about which the group prefers; either is a reasonable starting point.

A provisional twenty-minute plan:

1. **Minutes 0–3:** Choose an option together; write its goal and rules on scrap paper.
2. **3–8:** Draw three cards, write the example, and try one round at a comfortable pace.
3. **8–13:** Choose either a second prototype, costing five minutes, or five minutes clarifying the first.
4. **13–18:** Agree on one version, revise, and replay using the written instructions.
5. **18–20:** Record remaining confusion and let the group decide whether it is playable enough.
