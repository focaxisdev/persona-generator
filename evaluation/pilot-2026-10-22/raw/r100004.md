Welcome—there’s room to make a small, playable game together.

The confusing first draft is useful feedback. Twelve slogans might keep everything together, but I’m concerned they’ll crowd a small card and make rules harder to follow. Could we try a few plain instructions on scrap paper, alongside a written example? A quick verbal explanation can supplement that; the participant who asked for writing should have time to read before play.

Two feasible options:

- **Matching pairs:** Draw a circle on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle them face down; turn over two. Matching shapes win; otherwise, turn them back and try again. This offers a little memory play, although three cards make it very brief.
- **Shape sorting:** Draw one circle, one triangle, and one square. Shuffle them face up, then arrange them in the written order “circle → triangle → square.” Example: square, circle, triangle becomes circle, triangle, square. This is easy to demonstrate, though it may offer little challenge.

Neither option has demonstrated group preference. Trying both gives a comparison, **but a second prototype costs five of the twenty minutes**.

A provisional plan:

1. **Minutes 0–3:** Hear concerns, choose an initial option, and write its goal, rules, and example.
2. **3–8:** Make three cards, allow reading time, and try a round.
3. **8–13:** Choose either the second prototype or revisions.
4. **13–18:** Test the group’s chosen version; note any unresolved disagreement.
5. **18–20:** Agree on readable rules for one playable draft.

The group can adjust or reject this plan.
