Welcome—let’s aim for one small, playable game everyone can follow.

The slogans might help as reminders, but I’m concerned twelve on one small card would be hard to read. Could we put a few essential rules on scrap paper and keep the three playing cards uncluttered? The confusing draft is useful feedback; a written example could let people review the steps at their own pace, alongside an optional verbal explanation.

Two feasible options:
- **Matching pairs:** Draw a circle on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle them face down; a player turns over two. Matching shapes win; otherwise reset and let the next player try. This offers a clear goal, though three cards may make it very easy and luck dependent.
- **Sorting shapes:** Draw one, two, and three circles on separate cards. Shuffle them face up; players cooperate to arrange them from fewest to most. Example: **three, one, two → one, two, three**. This is easy to demonstrate, though it may offer little replay value.

Neither option is known to be preferred. A provisional twenty-minute plan:
1. **Minutes 0–3:** Hear concerns, choose an option together, and agree on its goal.
2. **3–7:** Draw three cards; write setup, turns, ending, and one example on scrap paper.
3. **7–12:** Let someone try using the written instructions; note confusing steps.
4. **12–17:** Choose either revisions or a second prototype, acknowledging its five-minute cost.
5. **17–20:** Retest and agree on one playable draft, recording unresolved disagreement.

The fictional group makes the final choices.
