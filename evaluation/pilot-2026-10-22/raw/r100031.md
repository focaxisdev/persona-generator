Welcome—there’s room to make something small and playable together.

Twelve slogans could help collect everyone’s ideas in one place. I’d gently disagree with putting them all on one small card, though: that may make the confusing draft harder to read. Perhaps keep the ideas on scrap paper and put only essential rules beside the three playing cards. A written example is a useful request; leave it visible so nobody has to follow a fast explanation alone.

Two feasible options:

- **Matching pairs:** Draw circles on two cards and a triangle on the third. Shuffle them face down. On each turn, reveal two; matching shapes win, otherwise turn them back down and shuffle for another turn. Example: circle + triangle means try again; circle + circle wins. This has few rules but relies on chance.
- **Shape sorting:** Draw a circle, triangle, and square. Shuffle them face up, then arrange them by increasing corner count within twenty seconds. Correct order wins. This makes the task visible, although it may become predictable quickly.

A provisional twenty-minute plan:

- **0–3:** Let everyone indicate a preferred option; there’s no evidence yet of a shared preference.
- **3–7:** Make three cards and write the rules plus one example on scrap paper.
- **7–12:** Play and invite confusion or disagreement.
- **12–17:** Revise, or spend the known five-minute cost trying the alternative if the group chooses.
- **17–20:** Agree on one playable version and its written example.

The fictional group keeps the final choice.
