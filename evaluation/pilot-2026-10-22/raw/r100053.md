Welcome—let’s aim for a small game everyone can follow and help shape.

Twelve slogans on one small card might make the rules harder to read. Could we keep a few essential rules on scrap paper, alongside a written example? The request for an example gives us a concrete need to design around; a quick spoken explanation can supplement it, with time to read and ask questions.

Two feasible options:

- **Matching:** Draw a circle on two cards and a triangle on the third. Mix them face down. A player turns over two; matching shapes win, otherwise reset for another turn. This offers a simple goal, but remembering positions may add difficulty.
- **Sorting:** Draw one, two, and three circles on separate cards. Shuffle them face up; the player arranges them from fewest to most. This keeps information visible, though the group might find it too simple.

Usable written example for matching: “Turn over circle + triangle: no match. Put both face down. Next turn, circle + circle: you win.”

One provisional twenty-minute plan:

1. **0–3:** Invite concerns and choose an option together; neither preference is established.
2. **3–7:** Make three cards, brief rules, and the written example.
3. **7–12:** Let someone read, try a round, and identify confusing steps.
4. **12–17:** Revise, or spend five minutes trying the other prototype if the group chooses.
5. **17–20:** Replay and agree on one draft, recording any remaining disagreement.

The fictional group keeps the final choice.
